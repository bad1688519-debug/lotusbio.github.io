Overview & mechanism

MOTS-c was described as a 16-amino-acid mitochondrial-derived peptide in a 2015 study of metabolic regulation. That work connected cell-signalling findings with mouse experiments. A later paper examined exercise-related observations and physical-capacity experiments, but the human and animal components answer different questions. [1] [2]

Selected research

These study summaries preserve the type of evidence and the question being tested. They are a curated starting point, not a systematic review of every publication.

2015 · Discovery and metabolic experiments

Cell systems and mouse models

The investigators described MOTS-c and reported effects involving metabolic signalling and insulin sensitivity in experimental models. [1]

Interpretation limit: Preclinical metabolic effects do not establish a clinical weight-loss or diabetes treatment.

2021 · Exercise and muscle-homeostasis research

Human exercise observations alongside mouse intervention experiments

The paper linked MOTS-c to exercise-related biology and reported physical-capacity findings in mice. [2]

Interpretation limit: The mouse intervention is not a randomized human trial of MOTS-c administration.

Is an exercise-associated biomarker evidence for supplementation?

A molecule changing after exercise does not show that administering it will reproduce exercise's benefits. An observational measurement can motivate a hypothesis without proving causation. In a paper containing both species, label each result by its model before comparing it: endogenous concentrations measured in people and an administered intervention in mice should never be collapsed into one claim about human treatment.

A practical reading checklist

  1. Identify the model: cell culture, animal experiment or human study.
  2. Name the comparator: placebo, another intervention or an untreated control.
  3. Record the endpoint: what changed, how it was measured and at which time point.
  4. Read uncertainty: consider sample size, missing observations and confidence intervals, not only a headline percentage.
  5. Keep the conclusion within scope: a mechanism, an analytical result and a clinical outcome are not interchangeable.

Material identity before comparison

Match the requested sequence and terminal modifications to the study material rather than relying on the phrase mitochondrial peptide. For metabolic assays, retain lot identity and the experimental system alongside the results. Do not import a research finding on a related analogue into the native sequence's record without explaining the structural difference. A supplier's label does not show that its sample reproduces a published biological response.

Batch quality & COA

Read a report as a set of measurements on an identified sample. First match the product, lot, sample identifier and test date; then ask what each measurement actually establishes.

Handling & research records

Use the handling instructions and safety documentation supplied for the exact material, presentation and lot. Do not copy a storage temperature or shelf life from a different formulation. Document receipt date, labelled storage requirements, container condition and any deviation for review under your laboratory's procedures.

Keep preparation records with the experimental dataset so that later results can be traced to a specific material. Appearance alone is not a stability measurement. If storage or shipping conditions are uncertain, request supporting information before treating the sample as suitable for the intended experiment. Explore storage documentation.

Common questions

Does MOTS-c replace exercise?

The selected research does not establish that claim. Exercise associations and animal experiments are not evidence of equivalence.

Does the 2021 paper demonstrate a human anti-aging treatment?

No. Human observations and mouse interventions must be interpreted separately; neither supplies that conclusion here.

Is an endogenous peptide automatically safe when supplied externally?

No. Identity, exposure, formulation and experimental context remain separate questions. Endogenous occurrence alone does not establish safety.

Does a research report establish safety for personal use?

No. Analytical quality, an experimental observation and clinical safety are different questions. This guide does not prescribe treatment or establish that research supplies are appropriate for human or veterinary use.
