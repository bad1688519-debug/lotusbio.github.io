How to Plan Private Label Peptide Packaging

LotusBio Editorial · 10 June 2026 · Updated 3 November 2026

A useful private-label packaging brief connects each product to its vial label, printed box and order quantity. Define the contents first, then request the packaging components and approvals needed for that specific project.

Choose the scope before requesting a quote

Project scopeInclude in the inquiry
Labels for research vialsProduct versions, vial measurements, label dimensions if confirmed, artwork status, quantity per design and application requirements.
Printed boxesVial count, intended layout, inside or outside dimensions, insert requirements and quantity per box design.
Labels and boxes togetherA product-to-artwork list linking each label and box version, plus the agreed variable fields.
Complete private-label supplyProduct codes and vial specifications, packaging components, applicable document requirements, destination and target delivery date.

Review the custom vial label page, printed packaging box page or OEM supply overview for the corresponding requirements. State explicitly if you want packaging without product supply; acceptance of that scope requires confirmation.

Keep vial, box and design quantities separate

For each product, record the vial specification, total vial count, vials per box and total box count. If several products use different artwork, state the quantities for each version instead of only a combined order total.

Planning example: 500 vials packed 10 per box require 50 filled boxes. If two products have 250 vials each, that is 25 boxes per product at the same packing count. These numbers illustrate the brief only; they are not a LotusBio minimum order quantity or availability promise.

Ask whether any additional labels, boxes or inserts are included in the quoted production quantity. Confirm how unused printed components and future artwork revisions will be handled.

Assign owners to the details that can change

Nominate who supplies and approves product text, artwork, variable batch fields and destination wording. Give files clear names that include the product, specification and revision. Keep label and box approvals connected to the same order line.

Agree who provides the dieline, prepares the final print files, applies labels and checks the completed packaging. Avoid assuming that all of those tasks are included in a price for a single component.

Distinguish artwork proofs, packaging samples and test vials

RequestPurpose and confirmation
Digital artwork proofReviews wording, layout and the exact file version. Use the artwork approval checklist.
Physical packaging sampleDiscuss review of the actual fit, closure, print presentation or label application. Availability, cost and timing require confirmation.
Research vial for testingA separate inquiry for material to send for independent testing. Confirm the product, vial quantity, applicable documents and shipping arrangements with the team.

Record which approvals are required before production. A digital proof does not replace a physical fit check, and a packaging sample does not establish a product test result.

Include destination requirements from the beginning

For international inquiries, specify the destination country, delivery details, requested languages and carton markings. Ask for the shipping terms and preparation timing that apply to your project. Have the appropriate reviewer assess wording and destination requirements before approving artwork.

Use the same structured brief for each market. Do not assume that a package approved for one destination has been reviewed for every other destination.

Check what the written quotation covers

  • Products and specifications, or a clearly identified packaging-only scope.
  • Dimensions, material, print finish and any insert for each component.
  • Quantities and minimum quantities by line or design.
  • Artwork preparation, proofs, sample review and application responsibilities.
  • Accepted file versions, change approval and applicable product documents.
  • Preparation timing, destination, shipping arrangements and quoted costs.
