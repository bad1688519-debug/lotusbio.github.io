What Is UBT-251? Clinical Evidence, Safety and the Retatrutide Comparison

LotusBio Editorial · Published 27 October 2026 · Evidence checked 26 October 2026

UBT-251, usually written UBT251 in scientific publications, is an investigational peptide that activates three receptors: GLP-1, GIP and glucagon. Researchers are studying its potential in obesity and type 2 diabetes. Its identity and receptor targets are described in the published first-in-human study.

For readers following metabolic research, the useful questions go beyond a headline weight-loss percentage. What was actually tested? How strong is the evidence? And does a comparison with another triple agonist tell us which performs better?

What does “triple receptor agonist” mean?

UBT251 is a single peptide candidate designed to act at three receptor types:

  • GLP-1: glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor.
  • GIP: glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide receptor.
  • Glucagon: the glucagon receptor.

“Triple” describes its receptor activity; it does not mean three separate medicines have been mixed together. The receptor profile provides a rationale for investigation, while clinical trials establish what happens in people. The phase 1a/1b publication examined safety, tolerability, pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamic effects.

UBT251 originated with The United Bio-Technology (Hengqin) Co., Ltd., a subsidiary of The United Laboratories. In March 2025, Novo Nordisk announced a licensing agreement covering development, manufacturing and commercialisation outside mainland China, Hong Kong, Macau and Taiwan; United Biotechnology retained those territories. This identifies the development partners, rather than establishing any relationship with independent peptide suppliers. Original licensing announcement.

What did the UBT251 phase 2 obesity trial show?

The October 2026 Cell Metabolism publication reported a randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled study involving 205 adults with overweight or obesity who did not have type 2 diabetes.

After 24 weeks, the statistical estimates of mean body-weight change ranged from −13.6% to −19.7% across UBT251 groups, compared with −2.0% with placebo. These were least-squares means, rather than a promise of what an individual would experience. Gastrointestinal symptoms were the most frequent adverse events and were generally mild to moderate. Published phase 2 results.

The practical interpretation is encouraging but limited: this trial supports further investigation in its studied population. It does not establish lifetime safety, sustained benefit after stopping treatment, or equivalent results in every patient group. The publication also discloses sponsor funding and company affiliations, which belong in an assessment of the evidence.

Is there also evidence in type 2 diabetes?

Yes, but the evidence source needs to be labelled clearly. On 25 March 2026, Novo Nordisk and United Laboratories announced topline findings from a separate study involving 211 Chinese adults with type 2 diabetes.

The announcement reported a maximum mean HbA1c reduction of 2.16 percentage points after 24 weeks, compared with 1.77 percentage points for the semaglutide 1 mg group and 0.66 percentage points for placebo. HbA1c is a measure of longer-term blood glucose control. Company announcement of diabetes results.

Here, the cited source is a company topline release, whereas the obesity section above cites a peer-reviewed publication. The specific comparator also matters: results against semaglutide 1 mg do not establish superiority over every semaglutide regimen, every outcome or every population.

UBT-251 vs Retatrutide: what is a fair comparison?

Both candidates act at GLP-1, GIP and glucagon receptors, but they are distinct molecules with separate development programmes. Their shared receptor class does not make them interchangeable.

Retatrutide's evidence base now includes the TRIUMPH-1 phase 3 trial, published in September 2026, with 2,339 randomised participants and 80 weeks of treatment. That is a different scale and duration from the UBT251 obesity study discussed above. Retatrutide phase 3 publication.

A useful comparison asks:

Questions for comparing clinical trials
QuestionWhy it matters
Were the participants comparable?Diabetes status and baseline characteristics can affect outcomes.
Was follow-up equally long?A shorter observation period cannot answer all longer-term questions.
Were outcomes analysed the same way?Missing data and treatment discontinuation can change the interpretation.
Was there a direct comparison?Separate placebo-controlled studies do not establish a head-to-head winner.

The sources reviewed for this article do not provide a direct UBT251-versus-Retatrutide trial. Calling one “better” on the basis of separate headline percentages would go beyond that evidence.

What is known about UBT251 side effects?

The first-in-human publication reported gastrointestinal adverse events and decreased appetite among the common findings. Safety and tolerability were its primary endpoints. Phase 1a/1b safety report.

For interpretation, frequency and severity should be considered alongside study size, follow-up, withdrawals and the population studied. A finding that treatment was generally tolerated in a monitored trial is not a guarantee of safety outside that setting.

Questions about uncommon adverse effects, prolonged exposure and outcomes in broader populations require evidence beyond an early trial. This article reviews the research; it does not provide dosing, combination or self-administration instructions.

Where is UBT251 being studied worldwide?

UBT251's development spans Chinese studies and an international clinical programme. The original agreement assigns United Biotechnology the rights in mainland China, Hong Kong, Macau and Taiwan, with Novo Nordisk holding rights elsewhere. These development territories help explain how the programme is organised; they do not establish local product availability. Development and licensing territories.

Novo Nordisk's second-quarter 2026 presentation described an ongoing global phase 1b/2a obesity programme, with results expected in the first half of 2027. It also reported initiation of a global phase 2 diabetes trial. These are development updates and expected timelines, not completed results. Company pipeline update, slide 41.

European sites are one documented part of this wider research activity. The official EU record 2026-525465-42-00, protocol NN9559-8577, lists a Novo Nordisk-sponsored phase 2 diabetes study with locations in Germany, Hungary, Romania, Spain, Poland and Slovakia. Official EU clinical trial record.

For readers in any region, three questions should be checked separately: where a trial is conducted, which population its results describe, and what the local regulatory status is. A trial location or a worldwide licensing agreement does not establish routine prescribing availability or marketing authorisation in a particular country. The same evidence standards apply wherever a researcher is based.

How should researchers evaluate a UBT251-labelled material?

A clinical publication and a laboratory certificate answer different questions. The publication concerns a defined investigational product in a specific study. A certificate of analysis concerns the submitted sample and the tests actually performed.

For material review, start with traceability:

  • Match the product name, sample identifier and batch or lot across the label and documentation.
  • Check which analytical methods were used and which attributes they assessed.
  • Read the laboratory's scope and limitations before interpreting a purity result.
  • Keep material identity, analytical purity and clinical evidence as separate conclusions.

A document bearing the name “UBT251” does not by itself demonstrate equivalence to the investigational product used in a clinical trial. Likewise, an analytical purity percentage alone does not establish suitability for human administration.
