Peptide Label & Box Artwork Approval Checklist

LotusBio Editorial · 13 May 2026 · Updated 3 November 2026

Before printing peptide vial labels or boxes, approve three things: the physical template, the product and batch information, and the exact artwork file. Use this checklist to separate design approval from decisions about fit, materials and application.

TemplateFile setupVariable fieldsApproval checklist

1. Start with the actual label or box template

Obtain the confirmed label dimensions or box dieline from the project team. A dieline describes the cut shape and, for a box, its panels and folds. Check the intended vial measurements before treating any downloaded template as a production template.

For a label, identify width, height, position and any wrap gap or overlap. For a box, clarify internal versus external dimensions and the insert layout. Use the vial label sizing questions and box fit checklist to complete missing details.

2. Agree on print file requirements

Ask the printer or production coordinator for the accepted file format, colour setup, image requirements and treatment of fonts and linked artwork. Supply the editable source if requested and a PDF for review; do not assume that a screenshot or a PDF alone is an approved production file.

Bleed extends artwork beyond the cut edge to avoid an unprinted border after trimming. Use the print provider's specified bleed and colour settings. Adobe explains these controls in its PDF marks, bleed and colour output guide; the required settings depend on the printing workflow.

Keep the dieline and production instructions distinguishable from the artwork. Ask which layers or marks should appear in the review proof and which belong in the final print file. Review fine text and contrast at the actual finished size.

3. Separate fixed artwork from variable information

Review groupQuestions for the artwork owner
Fixed layoutAre the brand name, graphic elements, contact information and intended-use wording the approved versions?
Product versionDo the full product name, catalog code, vial specification and box count agree across the label, box and order?
Batch informationWho supplies each variable value, where will it be printed, and how is it checked against the agreed product record?
Barcode or QRIs the encoded content final? Is the link intended for this product or report? Has readability been checked on the proposed printed presentation?
Language and destinationWho reviews the wording required for the intended market? Have translations and any destination-specific requirements been checked?

A private-label design must not turn a sample-specific test result into a claim about every batch. When linking a report, keep the report number and sample or batch connection clear; see how to review a COA.

4. Approve a named proof, not an informal preview

  • Confirm the finished dimensions and intended vial or box format.
  • Check every product variant, strength or specification and the quantity attached to it.
  • Review spelling, text size, panel orientation and reserved variable fields.
  • Check linked content and any barcode or QR requirements.
  • Record whether a physical sample is required for fit or material review; agree on availability, costs and timing.
  • Save the approved filename, revision, approver and date with the order.

A digital proof confirms the artwork under review. It does not demonstrate label adhesion, box fit or transport performance. Document those checks separately if they are part of the agreed project.

5. Reapprove changes before production

If a product name, dimension, variable field, material or print file changes, identify the affected versions and ask whether a new proof or sample is needed. Retain the previous approval for reference, but clearly identify which version is current.
