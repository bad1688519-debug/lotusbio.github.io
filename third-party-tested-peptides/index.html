What should you check before ordering?

Product and supplied form

Match the material identity, composition and specification to your inquiry. Keep finished-vial records separate from bulk raw-material records, and distinguish catalog codes from batch numbers.

Sample and batch identifiers

Read the sample reference, report number and any batch or lot identifier printed on the document. Ask which record applies to the proposed supply batch if that connection is not clear.

Test methods and results

Review the method, units, date and scope of each reported result. Chromatographic purity and measured content answer different questions; neither alone demonstrates sterility or endotoxin status.

Use the COA verification checklist →

Does a published COA cover the batch I will receive?

Only when the sample and batch information can be connected to the proposed supply. The public library lets you review existing records, while a quotation should identify the offered product, specification and relevant documents. Confirm this match before placing an order, including when reordering a previously tested product.

What if my required test is not listed?

Specify the test or document you need and the material format in your inquiry. Our team can review what is available for the proposed batch and clarify any gaps. An absent test result must not be treated as a passing result.

Connect the report to a product specification.

Compare Retatrutide vial specifications, Tirzepatide vial specifications and the research peptide catalog. For bulk quantities, use the raw-material catalog. Include the product code, quantity and document scope when you send a supply inquiry.
